# Groundless.ai vs the three FLF/FLI offerings

Archive note from the FLF Epistack contest Slack, 27 Aug 2026. Ankur Pandey
posted Groundless's Interface Alignment Slowathon into `#epistack-contest`.
This file is the background check and the category-by-category comparison
against the three Doctrine Labs FLF/FLI repos.

**Short answer:** same neighborhood, different job. None of Groundless's three
homepage categories is a product match for any of the three FLF/FLI offerings.

The honest overlap is one sentence both sides would sign: **the container is
doing more work than the content.** They mean frames and attention. We mean
citation structure and false independence.

## What they believe

Everything around AI is shaped by **frames** (what counts as a problem, what is
worth attending to). As models get stronger, the **interface** — the contact
point between a human and the system — is where harm actually lands. Three
threat modes from their Slowathon brief:

- **Hyperengagement** — pulled in further than you would choose.
- **Disengagement** — judgment and taste atrophy because the software does
  them.
- **Frame control** — the software decides what counts as a problem. Fixes at
  the level of *content* miss this.

Their slogan: *diffuse concepts for a diffuse response to diffuse risks.* They
argue you cannot write one formula for "deception" or "alignment," so they want
context-sensitive, per-person interfaces and "live theory" instead of a fixed
core template. They explicitly say they do **not** want autonomous "AI
Scientists."

## Their three homepage categories

From the **Diffuse Concepts** section of [groundless.ai](https://groundless.ai/):

1. **Modeling copilots** — AI that helps engineers apply theoretical frameworks
   to a specific design context without forcing a rigid formula.
2. **Connection interfaces** — custom interfaces that attune to *your*
   specifics instead of making humans bend to charging cables, network
   coverage, and bureaucratic standards.
3. **Empty Form** — alignment without fixed foundations. Values and facts are
   relational/contingent. Strategy is ongoing and responsive, not a permanent
   solution.

## Do any of the three categories align?

None of their three categories is a match for any of the three FLF/FLI repos
as a product.

- **Modeling copilots → weak overlap with Epistack only.** They want a copilot
  that applies theory to a specific context. Epistack does apply one theory
  (independence) to a specific corpus. That is where it stops. Their copilot
  is for *interface design*. Ours is a *mechanical auditor*. The load-bearing
  half of Epistack runs with no model. Their copilot *is* the model, kept
  flexible on purpose.
- **Connection interfaces → no match to the three FLF/FLI repos; real rhyme
  with Core OS.** They want software that attunes to one person and does not
  make you bend to the machine. The FLF suite is the opposite design: one
  general engine, three cases, least interpretation, same report format. The
  Core OS line in [`SUBMISSION.md`](../SUBMISSION.md) — AI in the loop to
  sharpen the human, local-first, gates on writes — is the actual cousin. That
  is not what we submitted to FLF/FLI.
- **Empty Form → mismatch, almost opposite method.** They refuse a fixed
  foundation. We shipped a fixed three-level model, eight mechanical checks,
  and a SPEC. The only shared restraint: we also refuse to "settle" COVID /
  LHC / eggs. We count structure; we do not claim the Answer. That is humility
  about *conclusions*, not emptiness about *method*.

Ankur's Groundless one-liner ("interfaces that reduce epistemic and AI-human
co-dependency") is closer to **Core OS** than to the three contest repos. That
is probably why he posted it in the epistack Slack: he sees co-dependency as
an epistemic problem. Our FLF answer to co-dependency is *don't let correlated
sources and correlated models count as independent*. Their answer is *rebuild
the contact surface so the frame cannot capture you*.
