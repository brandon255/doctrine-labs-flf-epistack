## Why steps failed — the part that matters

A pass rate on its own is unreadable, because "the model invented a citation"
and "the model paraphrased accurately" both fail verification while meaning
opposite things.

| Failure kind | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Quote not verbatim | 10 | Model summarised the block instead of quoting it |
| Cited a nonexistent block | 0 | Model invented a source |
| Cited nothing at all | 0 | Model asserted without pointing anywhere |

**Every single failure was paraphrase. The model never once invented a block id.**

That reframes the pass rate. The failures are not hallucination; they are a model
writing "the judge concluded roughly 1 in 300" where the block says "I concluded
that there is approximately a 1 in 300 chance". Substantively right, not verbatim.

We reject those anyway, and we think that is correct — a verifier that accepts
paraphrase cannot tell accurate paraphrase from convenient paraphrase, and the
whole value of stage 2 is that it does not have to make that judgment. But the
honest reading of "9/15" is "the conclusions were largely sound and the
quoting was sloppy", not "the model was unreliable".

The cost of that strictness is real and we pay it here: 10 steps with
sound reasoning were marked unverified for imprecise quoting. A larger model quotes
more faithfully, which is the cheapest available fix.
